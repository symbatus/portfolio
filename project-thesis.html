The Influence of the Media on the Formation of Public Opinion in the Context of Military Conflicts

Undergraduate thesis · Astana IT University · 2024

The question

How does the way a news story is framed affect how people understand a conflict?

Rather than looking only at whether a claim was true or false, we looked at how different outlets presented the same events: what they emphasized, who they held responsible, and what solutions they suggested.

The study

We analyzed 22 articles from eight news outlets across four countries:

  • Ukraine — 24 Kanal, TSN
  • Russia — Channel One, RIA Novosti
  • UK — BBC, The Guardian
  • Kazakhstan — Tengri News, Sputnik

The articles came from two periods: the first 12 weeks of the invasion and August 2022 – February 2023.

For each article, we used the same five-part frame matrix, looking at the main position, catch-phrases, problem source, implied solution, and how claims were presented.

What we found

Four main strategies appeared across the sample: discrediting, sequential information dissemination, fragmented dissemination, and disinformation. Discrediting was the most common, often appearing through negative evaluation, accusation, and threat.

We also found differences in how outlets presented the claims they reported. Seven articles explicitly endorsed the claims they presented, while 15 reported them without directly challenging them. None explicitly refuted the claims.

The outlets also differed in how they framed the conflict. Ukrainian outlets more often presented a clear solution, while British outlets tended to imply negative judgment without stating one directly. Russian outlets more often shifted responsibility for the problem, while Kazakh outlets tended to use more cautious wording.

From research to film

We also created a 3-minute 2D animated short inspired by the research. The film has no dialogue and explores the idea of how the same situation can be seen and understood differently.

What came next

The study was based on a relatively small sample, and manual coding also leaves room for interpretation.

It made me interested in how this kind of analysis could be made more systematic and applied to a much larger amount of media.

That question eventually led to Contexta — an attempt to turn part of the research framework from this thesis into a working AI tool.
